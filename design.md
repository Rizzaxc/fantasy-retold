# Fantasy Retold: System Architecture & Design Document

**Target Engine:** `pokeemerald-expansion`

Battle rules (Stamina, innate commands, Trauma, natures, status rails, held items in battle, the battle AI) are in [`combat.md`](combat.md). This document covers progression, overworld systems, trainer lineups and tooling.

---

## 1. System Philosophy & Overworld

*Fantasy Retold* transforms the traditional high-frequency switching, type-immunity-driven meta of vanilla Pokémon into an individual-focused, resource-managed tactical JRPG battle system. Battles are intentionally longer, more deliberate, and defined by individual matchup positioning and attrition management.

### Overworld Cadence

* **Accelerated Overworld Cadence:** Outside of battle, link / wireless, and test-runner sensitive paths, the main callback loop advances several logic updates per rendered frame, chosen by the **SPEED UP** row in the Option menu: **OFF** (1x), **2X**, or **4X** (new-game default 4X). It is stored in `gSaveBlock2Ptr->optionsSpeedUp` (2 spare option bits, `OPTIONS_SPEED_UP_*`) and mapped to updates per frame by `sOverworldLogicUpdatesPerFrame` in `main.c`. Before a save is loaded (title / intro) the field reads 0, so those screens run at 1x; saves made before this option existed also read 0 (OFF) until changed. Battle pace is not affected by this option. Music mixing and map-music cadence remain on the original VBlank schedule, so BGM tempo stays vanilla; **sound effects stay tied to game logic** and therefore accelerate with the faster simulation. The play-time counter also remains VBlank-based so the save clock tracks real time rather than simulation ticks. The Pokémon Center 1F's passive Union Room scan (`InitUnionRoom`, run from `CableClub_OnResume`) sets `gWirelessCommType` without any partner, so it does **not** count as a wireless session (`IsUnionRoomPassiveScanActive`): the overworld keeps the chosen speed inside Centers, while the extra ticks call the callbacks directly so RFU link servicing stays at 1x. Vanilla never shuts that scan down (map loads and menus wipe its task with `ResetTasks`), which left `gWirelessCommType` stuck at 1 after leaving a Center and pinned the overworld to 1x; `ResumeMap` now calls `StopUnionRoomPassiveScan` first, closing the scan's link, and the Center's OnResume script restarts it if the player is still inside. Menus also run at the chosen speed, so a load step can run again before VBlank has done the previous step's queued copy. Vanilla `LoadRegionMapGfx` (`region_map.c`) skipped the PokéNav map's tilemap and palette when that copy was still pending, which left the PokéNav map garbled; those steps now wait for the copy instead (the field map and Fly map load in one blocking loop, so only the palette step there goes ahead without waiting). The outdoor field-move banner (`FieldMoveShowMonOutdoors`, shown by Cut, Rock Smash, Strength, etc.) hides BG0 outside a window and only restored the window registers from its own VBlank handler. At 2X/4X, its RestoreBg and End steps can run in the same frame, so the restore never landed and BG0 (text boxes, Start menu, NPC dialogue) stayed hidden after any field move. `FieldMoveShowMonOutdoorsEffect_End` now writes WIN0H/WIN0V/WININ/WINOUT itself before handing VBlank back.

## 2. Expansion Mechanics

### Signature Moves

Every Pokémon can earn one Signature Move outside its four slots. Using it in battle (PP, Dodge, AI, trainer parties) is in `combat.md` §10.

*Current implementation:*

* **Mastering a move.** Every use of a slot move in battle counts toward mastering it, **per Pokémon and per move**, never per party slot or move slot: reordering moves, the party or the PC changes nothing. A move used `SIGNATURE_MOVE_USE_THRESHOLD` = 100 times is **mastered**. Only the player's own Pokémon count (not a multi-battle partner's), and only a move the Pokémon really has in that slot (not a Mimic / Transform copy). The Itemized Move and the Signature Move itself don't count.
* **Storage.** `SaveBlock1.moveUseRecords[PARTY_SIZE]` (`struct MoveUseRecord`): one record per Pokémon, keyed by personality, holding up to 4 move IDs with their counts (bits 0–6, saturating at 127; bit 7 = mastery announced). A Pokémon that needs a record takes a free one, else one of a Pokémon no longer in the party, else the least-used one, so a deposited Pokémon keeps its progress until its record is reused. A move it no longer knows gives its entry to a new move unless it is mastered. These records replaced the old per-slot counters (which leaked counts from one Pokémon to whoever took its party slot); every Pokémon started again from 0.
* **"Mastered" prompt.** Before a battle closes (not after a loss, nor in link / Frontier / e-Reader battles), each newly mastered move prints *"X mastered Y!"*, plus *"Once fully evolved, it can make a mastered move its Signature Move!"* if X isn't fully evolved (`TryAnnounceMasteredMove` in `HandleEndTurn_FinishBattle`, `BattleScript_FRMoveMastered` / `...NotFinal`). Once per move.
* **Committing it.** A Signature Move is **never assigned automatically**. When a Pokémon **evolves into its final stage** (evolution scene: level up, Rare Candy, items) with at least one mastered move it still knows and no Signature Move, the scene says *"X can commit a mastered move as its Signature Move!"* and opens the move selection screen (`ShowSelectSignatureMoveSummaryScreen_BW`; the row below the moves is "Cancel"). Only a mastered move can be picked (others play the error sound), then *"Make Y its Signature Move?"*: Yes commits it (*"Y became X's Signature Move!"*), No goes back to the selection. Cancel / B gives *"X didn't commit a Signature Move."* This runs before the evolution's new moves are learned. The move then **leaves the four-slot moveset**, freeing that slot (later moves, their PP and PP Ups shift up), and the Pokémon's move records start over (`CommitSignatureMove`). Trade evolutions don't offer it (a traded-in Pokémon has no mastered moves). Saves that still hold the signature in a slot are fixed up after battle and after evolution (`FixUpSignatureMove(s)`).
* **Earning one later.** A Pokémon that refused, mastered a move only after reaching its final stage (or never evolves), or forgot its Signature Move at the Move Relearner can commit one at the **Day-Care Man** for ¥5,000, under the same rule (§ Daycare Training, Signature Training).
* **Forgetting it.** The Move Relearner lists the Signature Move first on the level-up list (after the Tera Type entry, when the player has the Tera Orb) as *"Y (Sig.)"* (so `START RELEARN` / the NPC relearner show up for it). Picking it asks *"Make X forget its Signature Move, Y?"*; Yes clears it and the Pokémon's move records (*"Its uses count from 0 again."*). It is not put back into a slot; since it no longer counts as known, it can be relearned like any move. (`ForgetSignatureMove`, `move_relearner.c`.)
* **Knowing it.** The Signature Move counts as known: level-up learning, TMs and tutors treat it as already learned, so it can't be re-added to a slot.
* **Summary screen.** The BW summary's battle moves page is vanilla (four moves, `START RELEARN` at the bottom). **SELECT** swaps the four rows for the **innate moveset**, in the battle innate menu's order: Dodge, Recuperate (shown as *Innate*), then the Signature Move (type icon, name, PP `n/8` coloured like slot PP) and the Itemized Move (type icon, name, `4/4`), whichever it has; SELECT swaps back, and leaving the page resets to the four moves (changing Pokémon keeps the view). `A INFO` inspects them: the Signature / Itemized Move show the normal move details, Dodge / Recuperate a short description; the innate rows can't be reordered. SELECT does nothing on the contest page or in the forget-a-move / commit screens. A `SELECT INNATE` prompt sits at the bottom of the page, right of `START RELEARN` (or in its place when there's nothing to relearn); it's a sprite drawn in the relearn prompt's font and palette (`graphics/summary_screen/bw/innate_prompt.png`, `UpdateInnatePrompt`) and hides with it during move selection. (`showInnateMoves`, `GetBattleMoveRowMove` / `PrintBattleMoveRowDetails` in `bw_summary_screen.c`.)

### Itemized Moves

A held TM gives its move outside the four slots. Using it in battle is in `combat.md` §10.

*Current implementation:*

* **Slotting it.** Give a Pokémon a **TM** (held item) whose move it can learn (its teachable learnset). That move is its Itemized Move. The TM is **not consumed**: it is an ordinary held item, taken back like any other. HMs are key items and can't be held.
* **Who can hold it.** Giving a TM works like teaching one: the party shows **ABLE / NOT ABLE** on each Pokémon, and you pick its holder there. From the bag (*GIVE*) this is the usual "Give to which Pokémon?" screen; from the party menu (*ITEM → GIVE*), picking a TM in the bag opens the same screen with the cursor on the Pokémon you started from, and giving (or cancelling) returns to the normal party menu. Choosing a NOT ABLE Pokémon prints *"X can't learn Y, so it can't hold this TM."*; swapping held items between party members fails with the error sound. Other routes (PC item mode, Trick, Thief, ...) aren't blocked, but a TM the holder can't learn simply gives no move.

### Level-Band EXP

EXP rewards fighting opponents near your own level and discourages both over-levelling and power-levelling.

* **Rule.** Each EXP earner compares its level to the fainted Pokémon's. Within **±`EXP_LEVEL_BAND` (4) levels** (inclusive), its EXP is **doubled**, in wild **and trainer** battles. Outside the band, its EXP is **halved** in every battle (an award of 1 stays 1).
* **Why trainers too.** The doubling makes grinding less necessary: following the planned route, trainer fights alone keep the party in step with the level curve, so the player doesn't have to farm wild encounters to keep up.
* **Per earner.** The band is checked per Pokémon, so in one battle a Pokémon close in level can earn double while an over-levelled teammate or an Exp. Share holder earns half.
* **Engine.** This is the last step of `ApplyExperienceMultipliers` (`battle_script_commands.c`). It runs after the vanilla multipliers (traded, Lucky Egg, Exp. Charm, affection, Gen 5+ level scaling), so it stacks with them.
* **Summary screen.** The BW summary's skills page shows EXP only as the **bar**; the EXP total and "Next Lv." numbers are no longer printed (they read out of step with the bar). (`PrintExpPointsNextLevel` was removed from `bw_summary_screen.c`; the "EXP." label pill and the "/" between the numbers were erased from `graphics/summary_screen/bw/page_skills.bin`, rows 12–14, by swapping in the plain background tiles around them.)

### Gym Leader Rewards

After the badge and TM, each Hoenn gym leader offers **one extra reward, picked by the player from three**. All three are rolled up front and shown by name in the menu, so there is no mystery: the prompt is just *"You also get to choose one of these rewards."*

* **Held item.** A competitive held item, drawn evenly from one pool shared by every gym (`sGymRewardItems`, `src/gym_reward.c`):
  * *Power:* Choice Band / Specs / Scarf, Life Orb, Expert Belt, Muscle Band, Wise Glasses, Scope Lens, Razor Claw, King's Rock, Wide Lens, Zoom Lens, Metronome, Loaded Dice, Punching Glove, Throat Spray, Weakness Policy, Blunder Policy, Power Herb.
  * *Bulk / sustain:* Leftovers, Black Sludge, Shell Bell, Big Root, Assault Vest, Eviolite, Rocky Helmet, Focus Sash, Focus Band, Sitrus Berry, Lum Berry.
  * *Utility:* Quick Claw, Bright Powder, Air Balloon, Heavy-Duty Boots, Safety Goggles, Protective Pads, Covert Cloak, Clear Amulet, Ability Shield, Utility Umbrella, Mental / White / Mirror Herb, Red Card, Eject Button, Eject Pack, Room Service, Adrenaline Orb, Shed Shell, Toxic Orb, Flame Orb, Iron Ball.
  * *Field:* Light Clay, Terrain Extender, Binding Band, Grip Claw, Damp / Heat / Smooth / Icy Rock.

  Type-boosting items, Plates, Mega Stones, Z-Crystals and species-only items (Light Ball, Thick Club, ...) are left out.

* **Stat item.** Evenly one of **Bottle Cap** (spent on Hyper Training, § Daycare Training), **Ability Capsule**, **Ability Patch** (both upstream behaviour) or a **mint**. Mints change the **true nature**, innate included (§ Mints below). The mint is **Serious 10%** of the time (it stands in for every neutral nature); otherwise one of six innate groups, **15% each**, then evenly within the group:
  * *Standard offense:* Adamant, Modest, Jolly, Timid.
  * *Super Luck:* Lonely, Brave, Naughty, Mild, Quiet, Rash, Hasty, Naive.
  * *Battle Armor:* Bold, Impish. *Resilient:* Calm, Careful. *Regenerator:* Relaxed, Lax. *Natural Cure:* Sassy, Gentle.

* **Egg** of a random species that shares the gym's type, shown by species (`<SPECIES> EGG`). The gym types: Roxanne Rock, Brawly Fighting, Wattson Electric, Flannery Fire, Norman Normal, Winona Flying, Tate & Liza Psychic, Juan Water. The pool is every first-stage species (default form only, so no regional / Mega / battle forms) that:
  * has the gym's type itself (the hatchling, not a later evolution);
  * reaches **BST ≥ 500** (`GYM_REWARD_EGG_MIN_BST`): the highest BST among the final evolutions of its line (`GetLineBST`, shared with Lab Research), so Larvitar qualifies through Tyranitar, while Talonflame's line (499) does not;
  * is not restricted / sub-legendary, mythical, an Ultra Beast or Paradox;
  * is **not from Hoenn**: neither it nor anything it evolves into has a Hoenn Pokédex number (so Pichu is out, Pikachu being in the Hoenn dex);
  * isn't an Undiscovered-group species that never evolves (Unown and the like; babies are allowed).

  With a full party the Egg goes to the PC (and says so); only when the PC is full too is it refused.

**Flow.** The offer is a three-row menu (held item / stat item / Egg). It is rolled the first time the leader offers it and **kept until claimed**: **B** puts it off, and the leader offers the **same three** again whenever talked to, so backing out can't fish for a better roll. A full bag, or a full party *and* PC, says so and returns to the menu. It is offered once per gym (rematches don't repeat it).

* **Engine.** `Common_EventScript_GymReward` (`data/scripts/gym_reward.inc`), called with `VAR_0x8008` = gym number at the end of each gym's give-TM scripts and in the leader's post-battle talk once the TM is owned. Specials: `GymReward_IsClaimed` / `GymReward_SetClaimed` (`FLAG_GYM_REWARD_1`–`_8`, the old unused flags 0x21–0x28), `GymReward_RollOffer` (rolls the offer unless the stored one is this gym's, and buffers the names in `STR_VAR_1`–`3`; it runs again each time the menu shows, since `giveitem` overwrites them), `GymReward_GetHeldItem` / `GymReward_GetStatItem`, `GymReward_GiveEgg` (the stored species, via `ScriptGiveEgg`). The offer lives in `VAR_GYM_REWARD_GYM` (gym number it was rolled for, 0 once claimed), `VAR_GYM_REWARD_HELD_ITEM`, `VAR_GYM_REWARD_STAT_ITEM` and `VAR_GYM_REWARD_EGG_SPECIES` (the old unused 0x4091, 0x409B, 0x409D, 0x40A1); one offer is stored at a time, and an unclaimed older gym's offer is rerolled when it comes back. Stat item: `RollStatItem`, mint groups `sMintGroups`. Kanto (FRLG) gyms are not hooked.

### Mints

Mints change the Pokémon's **true nature**, not just its stat growth, so the nature's innate (`combat.md` §5) changes with it. This applies to every mint in the game, wherever it comes from.

* **Use.** From the bag, as upstream: *"It will change X's nature."*, Yes → *"X's nature became Y!"*. No effect only when both the true nature and the stat nature are already the mint's.
* **What is kept.** Gender, shininess, ability slot, Tera Type, IVs, EVs and everything else in the data; the old stat-only override (hidden nature) is reset to the new nature. Only cosmetics read from the personality's upper bits can change (Spinda's spots, and similar).
* **Engine.** `SetMonNature` (`pokemon.c`): shifts the personality by 256 × *k* (each step moves the nature by 6 mod 25, so every nature is reachable without touching the gender byte), rewrites it with `UpdateMonPersonality`, sets `MON_DATA_HIDDEN_NATURE` to the new nature and recalculates stats. Called from `Task_Mint` (`party_menu.c`). Mint descriptions read *"Changes nature; raises X and lowers Y."*

### Tera Orb

* **Roxanne's gift.** After her gym reward (§ Gym Leader Rewards), Roxanne gives the **TERA ORB** (Key Items). With it, one of the player's Pokémon can Terastallize **once per battle** into its Tera Type, as upstream (`CanTerastallize` looks for the orb in the bag). A full Key Items pocket says so; she offers it again whenever talked to (after the post-battle reward flow) until it is in the bag.
* **Using it.** On the move screen (or the innate menu, with Dodge / Recuperate) **START** toggles the Tera trigger next to the healthbox, as for the other gimmicks. It shows only while the player has the orb, hasn't used Tera yet this battle, and the Pokémon isn't holding a Mega Stone / Z-Crystal.
* **No recharging.** The orb is always charged (`B_TERA_ORB_ALWAYS_CHARGED` = `TRUE` in `include/config/battle.h`): no Pokémon Center visit is needed, the once-per-battle limit is the only one, as for Mega Evolution and Z-Moves.
* **Summary screen.** The info page shows the Pokémon's **Tera Type** icon (BW summary's built-in one, `BW_SUMMARY_SHOW_TERA_TYPE` = `TRUE` in `include/bw_summary_screen.h`), for every Pokémon, orb or not.
* **Changing it.** With the orb, the Move Relearner's level-up list (`START RELEARN` on the summary, or the relearner NPC) starts with **"Tera: <type>"**, so it is reachable for every Pokémon. Picking it lists the 18 types (not Stellar, not the current one) + Cancel; picking a type asks *"Change X's Tera Type to Y?"*, Yes sets it (*"X's Tera Type is now Y!"*) and goes back to the moves. Free and unlimited. The description window shows the type and a short explanation. Not offered for species with a forced Tera Type (Ogerpon, Terapagos). SELECT does nothing in the picker; B / Cancel goes back to the moves.
* **Tera Blast for all.** With the orb, **any** Pokémon that doesn't know **Tera Blast** finds it at the top of the Move Relearner's level-up moves (right after the Tera / Signature entries), whatever its learnset; it is learned like any relearned move. Species with a forced Tera Type get it too.
* **Engine.** `RustboroCity_Gym_EventScript_GiveTeraOrb` (`data/scripts/gimmick_reward.inc`, texts there too), called after `Common_EventScript_GymReward` in Roxanne's give-TM script and her post-battle talk. No flag: owning `ITEM_TERA_ORB` (a key item, never lost) closes the offer. Relearner: `CanChangeTeraType`, `teraPicker` / `SetTeraPicker` and the `Task_MoveRelearner_ChangeTeraType_*` tasks in `move_relearner.c`; the list ids are `RELEARNER_ID_TERA_ENTRY` / `RELEARNER_ID_TERA_TYPE_BASE + type` (`include/constants/move_relearner.h`), which `MoveRelearnerLoadBattleMoveDescription` (`menu_specialized.c`) and the hearts / category icon skip. `HasRelearnerLevelUpMoves` is TRUE with the orb. Tera Blast: `CanRelearnTeraBlast`, seeded first in `GetRelearnerLevelUpMoves`.

### Mega Ring / Z-Ring

The player gets **one** of the two gimmick rings, for good, and with it the items to use it.

* **Wattson's choice.** After his gym reward (§ Gym Leader Rewards), Wattson offers **both** rings and the player keeps **one**: `MEGA RING` / `Z-RING` (a two-option menu, then a YES/NO "you can't change your mind"). The ring lets the player's Pokémon Mega Evolve (holding a Mega Stone) or use Z-Moves / Ultra Burst (holding a Z-Crystal), as upstream (`CanMegaEvolve` / the Z-Move checks look for the ring in the bag). **B** puts it off; he offers it again whenever talked to (after the post-battle reward flow) until one is taken. A full Key Items pocket says so and leaves it open. The item `ITEM_Z_POWER_RING` is renamed **Z-Ring**.
* **Birch's call.** Once a ring is received, Prof. Birch **calls once** by PokéNav (after 10 steps in a town, city or route) and asks the player to come to the lab. His daily lab / Route 101 / Route 103 rotation is unchanged: talking to him **wherever he is** with the gift pending gives it, before the call too (then no call follows). While it is pending he is **also in the lab** whenever the player enters it, even on a route day.
* **Birch's gift** for the chosen starter (`VAR_STARTER_MON`): with the Mega Ring, the Mega Stone of its line (Sceptilite / Blazikenite / Swampertite; random if the line has several, e.g. Charizardite X / Y); with the Z-Ring, the type Z-Crystal of its first type (Grassium / Firium / Waterium Z). Bag full: come back later. A line with no Mega Stone gets an apology and closes the gift.
* **Game Corner.** The Mauville prize counter that sells the dolls also has **MEGA STONE (4,000 coins)** and **Z-CRYSTAL (3,000 coins)** rows, sold whichever ring the player holds. Each opens that category's **stock of 3**, shown by name (a bought one shows `SOLD OUT`); picking one asks to confirm, then checks coins and bag space.
  * *Stock:* 3 distinct items per category, uniform among the ones **not in the player's bag** when it is rolled (a stone held by a Pokémon doesn't count as owned). With fewer than 3 left, the rest of the slots are `SOLD OUT`. Mega Stones only count if some enabled species that is **not legendary** (restricted, sub-legendary or mythical) Mega Evolves with them (`FORM_CHANGE_BATTLE_MEGA_EVOLUTION_ITEM`), so Mewtwonite, Latiasite, Diancite etc. are out; every Z-Crystal (type and species) counts except the legendaries' (Mewnium, Tapunium, Solganium, Lunalium, Marshadium, Ultranecrozium Z).
  * *Refresh:* both categories reroll after every **gym badge**, **rival battle** and **Elite Four clear**, the egg shop's milestones (`GetEggShopMilestones`, now shared through `include/egg_shop.h`). Lazily, like the egg shop: checked when the prize lady is talked to, so several milestones between visits give one fresh stock; the first visit always stocks.
* **Overworld pick-ups.** Every Hoenn **vitamin** (HP Up, Protein, Iron, Calcium, Zinc, Carbos), **X item** (X Attack, X Defend, X Speed, X Accuracy, X Sp. Atk, Dire Hit, Guard Spec.) and **repel** (Repel, Super Repel, Max Repel; the Select menu's Infinite Repel makes them redundant) found in the field gives **one Mega Stone or Z-Crystal** instead: 50 spots, 31 item balls and 19 hidden items. These items are retired everywhere else too (§ Retired Items).
  * *Seeded table:* rolled from the trainer ID, so it is fixed for the save, never refreshed and stored nowhere. Each spot is a Mega Stone **2 times in 3**, else a **type** Z-Crystal (Normalium–Fairium Z only); Mega Stones come from the Game Corner pool (no legendaries). No item is seeded on two spots; if a category runs out, the spot takes the other one. Spots roll either category whichever ring the player has.
  * *Reroll on pick-up:* if the seeded item is already **owned** (in the bag or held by a party Pokémon), the spot gives another, picked uniformly from the first non-empty tier: (1) same category, not owned and not seeded on another spot that hasn't been picked up; (2) the other category, same rule; (3) same category, not owned but seeded on such a spot; (4) the other category, likewise. Nothing left: **Rare Candy**. The reroll is seeded by the trainer ID and the spot, so it changes only with what the player owns.
* **Engine.** `src/gimmick_reward.c` (+ `include/gimmick_reward.h`, `include/constants/gimmick_reward.h`) and `data/scripts/gimmick_reward.inc`. `VAR_BIRCH_GIMMICK_STATE` (the old unused 0x40FE): `BIRCH_GIMMICK_NONE` → `_RING` (ring taken) → `_CALLED` → `_GIFT_DONE`. Wattson: `MauvilleCity_Gym_EventScript_GiveGimmickRing`, called after each `Common_EventScript_GymReward` in the gym script. Gift: `ProfBirch_EventScript_GimmickGift`, reached from `LittlerootTown_ProfessorBirchsLab_EventScript_Birch` and `ProfBirch_EventScript_RatePokedexOrRegister` (route Birch) in state `_RING` or `_CALLED`; in those states the lab's OnTransition also clears `FLAG_HIDE_LITTLEROOT_TOWN_BIRCHS_LAB_BIRCH` after `ProfBirch_EventScript_UpdateLocation` (`..._ShowBirchForGift`), without hiding his route self. Special `BirchGimmick_GetGift` (walks the starter's evolutions for Mega Stones; type crystals are `ITEM_NORMALIUM_Z`–`ITEM_FAIRIUM_Z`, matched by `secondaryId`). ### Gauntlets

A **gauntlet** is a fixed run of trainer battles fought back to back with **no healing**, built to be very hard. Its battles pay almost **no EXP**, and clearing it pays a **one-time reward** instead. Winstrate is the first; more are planned.

* **Rules shared by every gauntlet.**
  * *EXP.* Any battle against one of the gauntlet's trainers (either opponent in a two-trainer battle) sets **every EXP award** to the gauntlet's `exp` (1 so far), for battlers and Exp. Share earners alike. It is the last step of `ApplyExperienceMultipliers`, after the level band (`GetGauntletExpOverride`). Prize money is paid as usual.
  * *Reward.* One item per **slot**, then **money**. A slot is a list of **groups**: the roll picks a group evenly, then an item in it evenly (a fixed item is one group of one item). It's rolled when given, and **all or nothing**: if the bag can't take every item, nothing is given and the map's claim NPC offers it again (rolled fresh, so nothing has been shown to fish for). Once given, it is claimed for good.
  * *Badge gate.* A gauntlet can require a gym badge (`requiredBadge`, 1–8; 0 = none). Without it, the first trainer refuses to start the run.
  * *Level scaling.* Its trainers' parties are authored for a **baseline badge count** (`baseBadges`). For every badge the player has beyond that, every Pokémon on every one of the gauntlet's trainers is **shifted up** by the gauntlet's step (`levelsPerBadge`), so coming back later stays hard. As for guest leaders, authored **EVs scale with the level** (each × new level / authored level; if a stat goes over 252 or the total over 510, all six are scaled down together so their ratio holds) and **moves are left as authored** (an empty move list uses the learnset at the new level). Levels are capped at 100. The shift is read from the badges held when each battle starts, and prize money is paid at the shifted level.
  * *Losing.* A whiteout anywhere in the run resets it: the map clears its trainers' flags while the last trainer is undefeated, and it starts again from the first.

### Guest Gym Leaders

From the **4th gym** on (Flannery, Norman, Winona, Tate & Liza, Juan), the **first** fight with the leader may host a **guest leader from another region**: Kanto for now (the 8 FRLG leaders). Johto joins once its leader sprites are in.

The guest is picked evenly among Kanto leaders whose **type differs from the home gym's** (no Blaine at Lavaridge, Sabrina at Mossdeep or Misty at Sootopolis) and who **haven't been beaten yet**: each guest can be beaten once per save. Nothing in the gym hints at it; the leader reveals the guest when talked to.
* **Format.** Rolled when the player engages, evenly: **tag team** (the guest, then the home leader, back to back with **no heal**; losing either is a whiteout), **doubles** (the guest and the home leader together, as a two-trainer double battle), or **guest only** (the guest stands in for the leader). The format stays **locked** until the player wins or whites out, so backing out and talking again can't fish for another. Mossdeep has no doubles (Tate & Liza already fight a double battle), so it rolls tag team or guest only, 50/50.
* **Two Pokémon.** Doubles, or a tag team that ends in Tate & Liza's double battle, needs two usable Pokémon when engaging; otherwise the leader says so and the player has to come back (the format stays locked). A double battle reached mid-tag with one Pokémon left still runs.
* **Guest teams.** Each guest has one party, authored at its Kanto levels with fully evolved Pokémon. At battle time every level is **shifted** by (home leader's highest level − guest's highest level), so the guest's ace matches the home ace and the spread is kept; moves are left as authored (an empty move list uses the learnset at the new level). Authored **EVs scale with the level** (each × new level / authored level); if a stat goes over 252 or the total over 510, all six are scaled down together so their ratio holds.
* **Strongest three.** In **tag team and doubles**, each leader (guest and home, Tate & Liza included) brings only their **3 strongest** Pokémon, in their party order. Strongest = highest level, then has a gimmick (Tera Type set, Dynamax / Gigantamax, or holding a Mega Stone / Z-Crystal / Primal orb), then has a held item, then most total EVs, then most total IVs, then random. Guest only uses the guest's full party.
* **Rewards.** Winning in any format gives the normal **badge, TM and gym reward**, plus the guest's **prize money** (paid at its shifted level; both leaders pay in tag team and doubles) and the **guest's TM** (`tm` in `sGuestLeaders`, set per guest; `ITEM_NONE` gives none). Rematches never have guests.

Region: `GetEggShopRegion`. The Hoenn rule is `IsInHoennFamily` (`gym_reward.c`, now shared through `gym_reward.h`). Prices are `sEggShopPrices`; slot *n* is tier *n*.

### Daycare Training

The Route 117 **Day-Care Man** trains party Pokémon for money. Talking to him opens `DAY-CARE` / `TRAINING` / `HYPER TRAIN` / `SIGNATURE` (B leaves; Hyper and Signature Training below); `DAY-CARE` is the vanilla flow (Egg pickup, checking on the mons).

* **Price.** **¥200 per level**, paid up front.
* **Cap.** He only trains up to **the party's highest level − 5** (Eggs don't count). A Pokémon already at or above that is refused, as are Eggs and every **Paradox** Pokémon (*"It'll have to grow in battle."*, § Lanette's Paradox Lab; `DAYCARE_TRAINING_PARADOX`). The Day-Care Lady won't take a Paradox to raise either (`Route117_PokemonDayCare_EventScript_RefuseParadox`, after the box picker, via `ParadoxLab_IsParadox`), so they only level in battle (or by Rare Candy). Hyper and Signature Training still take Paradoxes.
* **Flow.** Pick a party Pokémon; he offers `ONE LEVEL` or `TO LV. n`, where *n* is the highest level the player can afford, up to the cap (the message shows its price). The level up plays out like a **Rare Candy** on the party menu: stats window, level-up moves (with the replace-a-move prompt), then evolution. One Pokémon per conversation: backing out of the party menu or the offer, a refusal, or a finished training ends it.
* **Engine.** `Common_EventScript_DaycareTraining` (`data/scripts/daycare_training.inc`, texts there too), reached from `Route117_EventScript_DaycareMan`. Specials in `src/daycare_training.c`: `DaycareTraining_BufferOffer` (slot in `VAR_0x8004`; returns `DAYCARE_TRAINING_*` from `constants/daycare.h`, sets `VAR_0x8005` = affordable target, `VAR_0x8006` = level + 1) and `DaycareTraining_Charge` (takes the fee for slot `VAR_0x8004` → level `VAR_0x8005`). The level up is `DaycareTraining_LevelUpMon` in `party_menu.c` (it needs the Rare Candy flow's statics): it opens the party menu on the slot, sets EXP to the target level's threshold, and hands off to `Task_DisplayLevelUpStatsPg1` with `gSpecialVar_ItemId = ITEM_NONE` so the flow closes back to the script. Tunables: `DAYCARE_TRAINING_PRICE`, `DAYCARE_TRAINING_LEVEL_GAP`.

#### Hyper Training

The Day-Care Man also does **Hyper Training** (`HYPER TRAIN` on his menu).

* **Price.** **¥1,000 and one Bottle Cap per stat**, both consumed. The Bottle Cap can't be used from the bag (*"The Day-Care Man uses it to max out one stat's IV."*); gym rewards hand them out (§ Gym Leader Rewards).
* **No level requirement**, unlike upstream Hyper Training at Lv. 100.
* **Flow.** Without a Bottle Cap or the money he says so and ends. Otherwise pick a party Pokémon (Eggs refused, a Pokémon with every stat maxed is refused), then a stat: `HP` / `ATTACK` / `DEFENSE` / `SP. ATK` / `STAMINA` / `SPEED` / `CANCEL`, each row showing its IV or `MAX` (IV 31 or already trained). A maxed stat is refused. A YES/NO confirm with the money box, then *"X's STAT is now maxed out!"*.
* **Looping.** While the player still has a Bottle Cap and ¥1,000 he goes back to the stat list for the **same** Pokémon (to the party picker once it is fully maxed); B on the stat list goes back to the party picker, B there leaves. Out of caps or money, he says so and ends.
* **Effect.** Sets the stat's **hyper-trained flag** (`MON_DATA_HYPER_TRAINED_*`), which the stat calc reads as IV 31; the stored IV, and so Hidden Power, is unchanged.
* **Engine.** `Common_EventScript_HyperTraining` (`data/scripts/daycare_training.inc`, texts there too), reached from `Route117_EventScript_DaycareMan`. Specials in `src/daycare_training.c` (party slot in `VAR_0x8004`, stat in `VAR_0x8005`): `HyperTraining_CheckMon` (`HYPER_TRAINING_*` in `constants/daycare.h`), `HyperTraining_BufferStatRow` (row `VAR_0x8005` → `STR_VAR_2`, returns the stat; the rows are pushed with `dynmultipush`), `HyperTraining_CanTrainStat`, `HyperTraining_Train` (takes the fee and the cap, sets the flag, recalculates stats). Tunable: `HYPER_TRAINING_PRICE`.

#### Signature Training

The Day-Care Man also commits a **Signature Move** (`SIGNATURE` on his menu), for Pokémon that can't get one by evolving (§ Signature Moves).

* **Price.** **¥5,000**, taken on Yes. Without it he says so and ends.
* **Rule.** The same as on evolving into the final stage (`CanCommitSignatureMove`): the Pokémon is **fully evolved**, has **no Signature Move**, and the move is **mastered** and still in its four slots. Each failure has its own line, back to the party picker: Egg, not fully evolved, already has one (*the Move Relearner can make it forget that one first*), nothing mastered.
* **Flow.** Pick a party Pokémon, then one of its mastered moves (`CANCEL` / B goes back to the party picker), then a YES/NO confirm with the money box: *"Make Y X's SIGNATURE MOVE for ¥5,000?"* → *"Y became X's SIGNATURE MOVE!"*. The commit is the evolution one (`CommitSignatureMove`): the move leaves its slot, PP 8/8, the move records start over. One Pokémon per conversation.
* **Engine.** `Common_EventScript_SignatureTraining` (`data/scripts/daycare_training.inc`). Specials in `src/daycare_training.c` (party slot in `VAR_0x8004`, row among the mastered moves in `VAR_0x8005`): `SignatureTraining_CheckMon` (`SIGNATURE_TRAINING_*` in `constants/daycare.h`; `VAR_0x8006` = mastered move count, for the `dynmultipush` loop), `SignatureTraining_BufferMove`, `SignatureTraining_Commit` (re-checks the rule and the money). Tunable: `SIGNATURE_TRAINING_PRICE`.

### Mart TMs

Every Hoenn **town mart** (the clerk in each `*_Mart`: Oldale, Petalburg, Rustboro, Slateport, Mauville, Verdanturf, Fallarbor, Lavaridge, Fortree, Mossdeep, Sootopolis) sells **5 TMs** after its normal stock, at a flat **¥5,000** each.

* **Owned TMs hidden.** A TM the player owns (§ Reusable TMs) is left out of every buy list, these marts and Lilycove alike, so a town mart can list fewer than 5. The slots are not refilled.
* **Evolution items.** After its TMs, each of these marts also sells **3 evolution items** at **¥5,000**, from the classic evolution pool (§ Reusable TMs), fixed per save the same way: one shuffle seeded by the trainer ID, 3 distinct per mart, sliced in story order, wrapping around. They always show, owned or not.
* **Fixed per save.** The TMs are rolled once per playthrough and never refresh: they are derived from the **trainer ID**, so nothing is stored and a new game rolls new ones. They show from the first visit, whatever the mart's basic / expanded stock.
* **Pool.** The 50 TMs minus the **8 gym TMs** (Rock Tomb, Bulk Up, Shock Wave, Overheat, Facade, Aerial Ace, Calm Mind, Water Pulse), which stay gym-only: 42 TMs. HMs are never sold.
* **Spread.** The pool is shuffled once (seeded by the trainer ID) and each mart takes the next 5 in **story order** (Oldale first, Sootopolis last), wrapping around. So a mart never lists the same TM twice, the first 8 marts share none, and only the last 3 (Fortree, Mossdeep, Sootopolis) repeat TMs from earlier marts (55 slots for 42 TMs).
* **Price.** ¥5,000 in these marts only (Slateport's PokéNews sale halves it, as any mart price); the TM's own price is unchanged elsewhere (Lilycove, selling).
* **Names.** Every mart's buy list shows a TM with its move, **`TM08 Bulk Up`** (the list font narrows to fit), not just `TM08`. This also applies to the Lilycove Department Store TM counters.
* **Engine.** `src/shop.c` + `include/constants/mart_tms.h`. Each clerk script runs `setvar VAR_0x8004, MART_TMS_<TOWN>` + `special PokeMart_SetTMStock` before its `pokemart`; the special leaves the id pending and `CreatePokemartMenu` moves it into `sMartInfo.tmStockId` (`SetShopTMStock`; the decoration shops clear it). It is taken there, not in `CreateShopMenu`, because that one also reopens the BUY / SELL menu after the buy or sell screen, which would drop the TMs. `CB2_InitBuyMenu` runs `BuyMenuAppendMartTMs` after `TryBuildDynamicShopItemList`, replacing that allocated list with one holding the 5 TMs (`GetMartTMs`: pool = `FOREACH_TM` minus `sMartTMExcluded`, Fisher-Yates with `LocalRandomSeed(OTID ^ "MART")`, slice `(id - 1) * 5`). Every buy-menu price goes through `BuyMenuGetItemPrice` (`MART_TM_PRICE` for a TM while `tmStockId` is set). `BuyMenuSetListEntry` appends the move name to TM / HM names (`sItemNames` widened by `MOVE_NAME_LENGTH`). `BuyMenuFreeMemory` recounts `itemCount` from the restored script list after `TryFreeDynamicShopItemList`, so reopening BUY in the same visit doesn't read past the list end (the count still held the +5 TMs). Owned TMs: `BuyMenuAppendMartTMs` drops them (`PlayerOwnsItem`), `TryBuildDynamicShopItemList` (`shop_criteria.c`) drops them from script lists, and `IsSoldOutForPlayer` shows a TM bought this visit as SOLD OUT and sells TMs one at a time. Evolution items: `GetMartEvoItems` (`LocalRandomSeed(OTID ^ "MEVO")` over `GetClassicEvoItem`, slice `(id - 1) * 3`), priced `MART_EVO_ITEM_PRICE` by `BuyMenuGetItemPrice` through `IsMartEvoItem`. No vars or flags.

### Reusable TMs

* **Rule.** Teaching a TM never uses it up (party menu and Move Relearner alike), so the player needs **one copy of each**. TMs stay ordinary, holdable items: giving one to a Pokémon (an Itemized Move, § Itemized Moves) takes that copy out of the bag, and it can't be taught until it is taken back. TMs can't be **sold** or **tossed** (bag, shop SELL, PC); they can still be deposited in the PC. The TM pocket shows no count. HMs are key items, as before. A held TM is never lost in battle: held items come back after it (`combat.md` §9).
* **Owned** means in the bag, in the PC, or held by any party, boxed or daycare Pokémon.
* **No duplicates.** An owned TM disappears from every shop (§ Mart TMs). An item ball, hidden item or scripted gift (gym leaders included) that would give an owned TM gives a **random classic evolution item** instead, rolled with `Random()` at pick-up. A scripted gift first says *"Oh, you already have TM24 Thunderbolt? Then take this Fire Stone instead!"*.
* **Classic evolution pool.** The 10 stones (Fire, Water, Thunder, Leaf, Moon, Sun, Shiny, Dusk, Dawn, Ice), Linking Cord, Metal Coat, King's Rock, Dragon Scale, Up-Grade, Dubious Disc, Protector, Electirizer, Magmarizer, Reaper Cloth, Razor Claw, Razor Fang, Oval Stone, Prism Scale, Deep Sea Tooth, Deep Sea Scale, Sachet, Whipped Dream. The town marts sell from it too (§ Mart TMs).
* **Linking Cord.** Besides its upstream evolutions, it runs every **trade evolution** (`EVO_TRADE`): plain ones, and held-item ones when the Pokémon **already holds** the item (Onix + Metal Coat, Poliwhirl + King's Rock, Dusclops + Reaper Cloth, Clamperl + Deep Sea Tooth / Scale, Feebas + Prism Scale...). The held item is used up, as in a trade, and so is the Cord. Evolutions that need a specific trade partner (Karrablast / Shelmet) still need a real trade.
* **Engine.** `IsReusableTM` / `PlayerOwnsItem` (`item.c`). Not consumed: `Task_LearnedMove` (`party_menu.c`), `ShouldConsumeTmItem` (`move_relearner.c`). Sell / toss: `Task_ItemContext_Sell` (`item_menu.c`), `ItemStorage_DoItemToss` (`player_pc.c`); the TM pocket never had TOSS. Duplicates: `src/reusable_tm.c` (`sClassicEvoItems`, `FilterObtainedItem`, special `ObtainItem_FilterTM`), run at the top of `Std_ObtainItem` (with the line, `EventScript_AlreadyHaveTM`) and `Std_FindItem` (item balls, `finditem`), and on hidden items in `GetInteractedBackgroundEventScript` (`field_control_avatar.c`). Linking Cord: the `EVO_TRADE` case in `GetEvolutionTargetSpecies`'s item-use branch (`pokemon.c`); `DoesMonMeetAdditionalConditions` removes the held item for `IF_HOLD_ITEM`, and gets no trade partner, so `IF_TRADE_PARTNER_SPECIES` fails.

### Lab Research Trade

Prof. Birch's **Aide** in the Littleroot lab runs a 2-for-1 trade: the player lends him **two party Pokémon** for research and gets **one** stronger, foreign Pokémon back.

* **When.** The first request opens with the Route 103 rival win: at the end of the Pokédex scene the Aide walks up to the player, explains the trade and says he'll have a new request after every gym badge (he doesn't offer it there; the player only has the starter). After that, one more request opens after every **gym badge** and every **Elite Four clear** (Hall of Fame entry). Missed ones don't stack: several milestones between visits give one request. Later rival battles and gym rematches don't count. With no request open he only chats; later requests start by talking to him, or by **PokéNav call**: once the player has the PokéNav (`FLAG_SYS_POKENAV_GET`), the Select menu gets a **LAB RESEARCH** row that calls him and runs the same trade from anywhere. To make the player aware of it, he **calls once** after the PokéNav is received (after 10 steps in a town, city or route) to point them to that row; he never calls about individual requests.
* **Inputs.** Picked one at a time from the **party** (B on either pick backs out). Not an Egg, not the same Pokémon twice; fainted Pokémon and any level are fine, and a 2-Pokémon party can trade (it ends with 1). Held items go back to the bag (with the Pokémon if the bag is full).
* **Type request.** From the **4th** request on, he names a type: at least one input must have it (its current types). Rolled once when the request opens (talking again doesn't change it), uniform over the 18 types, never the same as the last request. It doesn't touch the reward roll.
* **Reward type.** A type is rolled from the inputs' current types, duplicates kept (Pidgey + Zubat = Normal, Flying, Poison, Flying: Flying 50%). The reward's line must have that type in **some member**. A type with no eligible species is dropped and the roll repeats.
* **Reward species.** Uniform among the eligible species of that type:
  * **first stage** (babies count), in its default form or a **regional form** (Galarian Meowth, Hisuian Growlithe, Paldean Wooper...), and the default form must be a first stage too (no battle forms);
  * **not from Hoenn** (the gym Egg rule, `IsInHoennFamily`), not restricted / sub-legendary, mythical, Ultra Beast, Paradox or Totem; Undiscovered-group lines (Unown...) are allowed;
  * **line BST** above both inputs' line BSTs. A species' line BST is the best BST among the final evolutions it can reach (default forms, no Megas), its own if it doesn't evolve. Strictly higher, with no relaxation at the top: two 600-class Pokémon can never be traded.
* **The reward** is a Lv5 gift (player OT, Poké Ball, met in Littleroot, normal shiny odds, registered in the Pokédex): random nature and non-hidden ability, and **each IV is rolled twice, keeping the higher**. It is rolled at confirmation, with no preview, then offered a nickname.
* **Order.** Pick both, then he checks the pair before anything is taken (type request, then whether any reward exists), then confirms (*"I'll take X and Y..."*), then swaps them.
* **No reward possible.** He offers a **Rare Candy** instead (the player may decline and keep the request open). Taking it uses up the request and counts as a completed one (toward the 4th-request type rule); with a full bag the request stays open. Missing the requested type has no such skip.
* **Engine.** `src/lab_research.c` + `Common_EventScript_LabResearch` (`data/scripts/lab_research.inc`), reached from `LittlerootTown_ProfessorBirchsLab_EventScript_Aide` once `VAR_BIRCH_LAB_STATE` ≥ 5 (Pokédex received); the walk-up is `LittlerootTown_ProfessorBirchsLab_EventScript_AideExplainResearch`, at the end of `..._GivePokedex` (the Aide is held still at (9,8) for that scene by `..._SetAidePosForPokedex` on transition). Vars (the old unused 0x40FB–0x40FD): `VAR_LAB_RESEARCH_KEY` = milestones (badges + `GAME_STAT_ENTERED_HOF`) at the last completed request, `VAR_LAB_RESEARCH_COUNT` = completed requests (the first is open while it is 0), `VAR_LAB_RESEARCH_TYPE` = requested type in the low byte, bit 15 while the open request has rolled it (cleared when a request completes; the low byte stays as the last type), bit 14 once the Aide has made his one PokéNav call (never cleared). Specials: `LabResearch_GetState` (`LAB_RESEARCH_CLOSED` / `_OPEN` / `_OPEN_TYPED`, type name in `STR_VAR_1`), `LabResearch_CheckPair` (`LAB_RESEARCH_PAIR_OK` / `_NEEDS_TYPE` / `_NO_REWARD`; slots in `VAR_0x8005` / `VAR_0x8006`), `LabResearch_Trade` (rolls, swaps, returns the reward's slot, also in `VAR_0x8004`, species in `STR_VAR_2`), `LabResearch_Skip`. Constants in `include/constants/lab_research.h`. The PokéNav call is `Common_EventScript_LabResearchCall` (from `Common_EventScript_SelectMenu`); it sets `VAR_0x8009` so the script re-locks with `lockall` after each party pick instead of `lock` / `faceplayer`. The incoming call is `ShouldDoLabResearchCall` (`include/lab_research.h`, step counter in EWRAM), checked with the vanilla scripted calls (Scott, Roxanne...) in `TryStartStepCountScript` (`field_control_avatar.c`), which runs `Common_EventScript_LabResearchIncomingCall` (`pokenavcall`, then `LabResearch_SetCalled`).

### Lanette's Paradox Lab

**Lanette** (Route 114, her house) works on Paradox Pokémon once the player has **badge 5** (Norman). Before that she is vanilla (the Lotad Doll, then her Storage System advice).

* **Gift (once per save).** The first talk with badge 5 (after the doll, if it was still owed; with no room for the doll she says so and goes on) gives **one random Paradox**: evenly one of the 14 **non-legendary** ones: Great Tusk, Scream Tail, Brute Bonnet, Flutter Mane, Slither Wing, Sandy Shocks, Roaring Moon, Iron Treads, Iron Bundle, Iron Hands, Iron Jugulis, Iron Moth, Iron Thorns, Iron Valiant. The legendary-based six (Walking Wake, Gouging Fire, Raging Bolt, Iron Leaves, Iron Boulder, Iron Crown) are never given; the engine doesn't flag them legendary, so the pool is a fixed list. Rolled at the handover, no preview. **Lv10**, random nature, plain random IVs and ability (they have one), player OT, Poké Ball, met on Route 114, normal shiny odds, registered in the Pokédex, nickname offered. Party, else PC; with both full she says so and gives it on a later talk (rolled again).
* **Why Lv10.** These are 570–590 BST Pokémon arriving around Norman (33–36): the player has to raise them, and neither the Day-Care Lady nor the Day-Care Man will (§ Daycare Training). Level-band EXP (§2) halves what a Lv10 earns against Lv30+ foes.
* **Conversion.** Every later talk opens `CONVERT` / `ADVICE` / `CANCEL` (B leaves). `CONVERT` picks a **party** Pokémon (B goes back to the menu); only the **exact canon species** convert (not the rest of their lines): Donphan → Great Tusk **or** Iron Treads, Volcarona → Slither Wing **or** Iron Moth (for these two the player picks either one by name, or `CANCEL`), Jigglypuff → Scream Tail, Amoonguss → Brute Bonnet, Misdreavus → Flutter Mane, Magneton → Sandy Shocks, Salamence → Roaring Moon, Delibird → Iron Bundle, Hariyama → Iron Hands, Hydreigon → Iron Jugulis, Tyranitar → Iron Thorns, Gardevoir / Gallade → Iron Valiant. An Egg, a Pokémon with no Paradox or one that already is a Paradox each get their own line, back to the picker. A YES/NO warns it forgets every move, the Signature Move too, and can't be undone; then a fade with the PC sound and *"X turned into Y!"* (evolution fanfare). **Free, unlimited, one per conversation, one-way** (no Paradox → normal).
* **What carries over.** The Pokémon is changed in place: **level, nature (and mint), EVs, IVs**, hyper-training, personality / shininess, OT, ball, held item, friendship, Tera Type, status and current damage stay. The EXP is set to the start of its level on the Paradox's growth rate; the ability becomes the Paradox's (Protosynthesis / Quark Drive; the nature innate is unchanged); stats are recalculated. An un-nicknamed Pokémon takes the new species name. Registered in the Pokédex.
* **What is wiped.** All four slot moves and their PP Ups, the **Signature Move** and the Pokémon's move records (mastery starts over). The slots get the Paradox's level-up moves for its level, as a freshly made Pokémon; the Move Relearner does the rest.
* **Booster Energy.** Every **Quark Drive** Pokémon she hands over (the gift or a conversion: the Iron ones) comes with a **Booster Energy**, every time. With a full bag the item is lost (the usual "no room" message); the Pokémon is still given.
* **Engine.** `src/paradox_lab.c` + `include/constants/paradox_lab.h`, scripts and texts in `data/maps/Route114_LanettesHouse/scripts.inc`. `FLAG_RECEIVED_PARADOX_GIFT` (the old unused 0x3A). Specials (party slot in `VAR_0x8004`, pick in `VAR_0x8005`): `ParadoxLab_RollGift` (species, name in `STR_VAR_1`; given with `givemon` at `PARADOX_LAB_GIFT_LEVEL`), `ParadoxLab_CheckMon` (`PARADOX_LAB_MON_*`), `ParadoxLab_BufferTarget`, `ParadoxLab_Convert` (`ForgetSignatureMove`, species, EXP, ability slot 0, `GiveMonInitialMoveset`, `CalculateMonStats`, `EvolutionRenameMon`, Pokédex), `ParadoxLab_IsQuarkDrive` (species in `VAR_0x8006`). Source table `sParadoxSources`, gift pool `sParadoxGiftPool`; disabled species are skipped.

### Select Menu (Infinite Repel, Exp. Share)

Pressing **SELECT** in the overworld opens a small menu (it replaces vanilla's direct use of the registered key item, which is now its third entry). Rows: **REPEL: OFF/ON**, **EXP. SHARE: OFF/ON**, **KEY ITEM**, **LAB RESEARCH** (only once the player has the PokéNav: calls Birch's Aide for the research trade, see Lab Research Trade), **CANCEL**. A toggle relabels the row and the menu stays open with that row selected; **B** closes it. The Mom in Littleroot explains it when she hands over the Running Shoes (`LittlerootTown_Text_ExplainSelectMenu`). The start menu no longer has a Repel entry.

* **Infinite Repel.** While ON it works like a Repel that never wears off: wild Pokémon below the lead's level don't appear (same rule as the item, `IsWildLevelAllowedByRepel`), including overworld-spawned encounters. Repel and Lure items keep working on top of it.
* **Exp. Share.** While ON, every healthy party member that did not fight earns **half** the EXP of the fainted foe; the ones that fought keep their full share. This is upstream's Gen 6+ Exp. Share (`IsGen6ExpShareEnabled`, `expShareExpValue` = half), driven by `I_EXP_SHARE_FLAG` = `FLAG_EXP_SHARE`, so the other EXP rules still apply on top (the level band, §2). The held Exp. Share item (Gen 5 style) still works too.
* **KEY ITEM.** Runs the registered key item as Select used to. The entry queues it (`SelectMenu_QueueRegisteredItem`) and the field input handler runs `UseRegisteredKeyItemOnField` once the menu script has ended; with nothing registered, the usual "no registered item" message shows. Nothing opens in the Union Room or a multi-partner room (as vanilla).
* **Engine.** `Common_EventScript_SelectMenu` (`data/scripts/select_menu.inc`, a `dynmultichoice` whose labels come from `SelectMenu_BufferLabels`), opened from `ProcessPlayerFieldInput` (`field_control_avatar.c`); specials in `src/select_menu.c`. `FLAG_INFINITE_REPEL` (was `FLAG_UNUSED_0x020`) and `FLAG_EXP_SHARE` (was `FLAG_UNUSED_0x029`) are saved flags. `IsRepelActive()` (`wild_encounter.c`) = Repel steps left **or** the flag.
### HMs Without Teaching

* **Rule.** The badge-gated HM field moves (Cut, Flash, Rock Smash, Strength, Surf, Fly, Dive, Waterfall) can be used with **no Pokémon knowing the move**. The matching **badge is still required**. A Pokémon that does know the move is still preferred as the user. Teleport, Dig, Sweet Scent, Soft Boiled and the other non-badge field moves are unchanged.
* **Flag.** `OW_HM_WITHOUT_TEACHING` (`include/config/overworld.h`), currently **TRUE**. Setting it to FALSE restores the vanilla "a party member must know it" rule.
* **User.** With nobody knowing the move, the first non-egg party member performs it (its name and sprite appear in the "X used CUT!" text and animation).
* **Engine.** `IsFieldMoveFreeToUse` (`field_move.h`) = flag on, `BADGE_UNLOCK` move, badge owned. `ScrCmd_checkfieldmove` falls back to the first non-egg mon when no one knows the move, which covers every script-driven use (Cut tree, Rock Smash, Strength, Waterfall, Dive). `PartyHasMonWithSurf` returns TRUE for Surf. Cut (grass), Flash and Fly have no overworld trigger, so `SetPartyMonFieldSelectionActions` adds them to **every** party member's menu while the badge is owned and there is room (the menu fits Summary, Stat Edit, 4 field moves, Switch, Item, Cancel; moves the Pokémon really knows are listed first).
* **Cut on grass ends its script.** Using Cut on grass from the party menu runs `EventScript_CutGrassCommon`, which waits (`waitstate`) on the grass effect. Upstream never resumed it: `CutGrassSpriteCallbackEnd` only unlocked controls natively, leaving the global script context stuck partway through a `lockall` script with `FLAG_SAFE_FOLLOWER_MOVEMENT` set. Now the effect's end (and the Ruin Valley dotted-hole branch) calls `ScriptContext_Enable`, and the script ends with `releaseall` / `end`. Mew's Faraway Island hide-and-seek still replaces it with its own script. (The invisible text/menu bug after field moves was a separate speed-up issue; see §1, Accelerated Overworld Cadence.)
* **Itemized and Signature field moves.** Independent of the flag, a field move a Pokémon has **outside its four slots** counts for the overworld: its **Signature Move**, or its **Itemized Move** (a held TM it can learn, e.g. a Dig TM gives Dig). `MonCanUseFieldMove` (`party_menu.c`) = `MonKnowsMove` (slots + Signature) or `GetItemizedMove`; `ScrCmd_checkfieldmove` and `PartyHasMonWithSurf` use it, and the party menu lists such moves after the slot moves (same 4-entry cap, shared with the free HMs above). `MonKnowsMove` itself is unchanged, so teaching a TM the Pokémon already holds is still allowed.

## 4. Development Tooling

### Design docs

* **`combat.md`**: everything that changes what happens inside a battle (§1–9 kept the numbers they had in the old combined document; §10 Signature / Itemized Moves in battle, §11 Smart Trainer AI, §12 other battle rules, §13 battle tuning constants).
* **`design.md`** (this file): progression, overworld systems, trainer lineups, tooling.
* Code comments cite `combat.md §N` or `design.md §N`. `AGENTS.md` tells agents to read both and to document a feature in the one it fits.

### Challenge-team skill

`.claude/skills/challenge-team/` is an agent skill for designing and reviewing boss teams (gym leaders and rematches, rivals, guest leaders, gauntlets, Elite Four / Champion) under the battle rules. It is about gameplay, not legality.

* **Inputs.** Format (single / double / tag team), the imposed limitation or archetype, the mon profile (how many, pushed toward power / bulk / speed), the player level, side notes.
* **Assumptions.** A hardcore player who has every option; a maxed AI team (31 IVs, full EV budget, every gimmick the slot allows) on `AI: Smart Trainer`; only builds the AI can pilot. A plan that needs a simple AI behaviour the AI doesn't have yet is kept, and the AI fix is proposed as a separate task.
* **Output.** A short team (species, gimmick, role, what makes each pick good), then the analysis: threat profile per mon, pressure map and deliberate holes, type analysis under the 2× cap (with rail interactions), support coverage, 3–4 plausible player teams with a projection each, verdict and tuning knobs. On approval it writes the `trainers.party` blocks and updates §2.
* **Files.** `SKILL.md` (inputs, workflow, quality bar), `references/meta.md` (how the rules reshape stats, natures, roles, types and formats; what the AI can pilot), `references/output-template.md`. Rules are always read live from `combat.md` and `design.md`; `meta.md` only interprets them and must be fixed if they drift.

### clangd / Zed include-path fallback

This repo now includes a root `.clangd` file so editor tooling can resolve the main game headers even though `bear`'s generated `compile_commands.json` only captures helper-tool builds under `tools/` and not the ROM `src/*.c` pipeline.

The fallback flags mirror the minimum project assumptions needed for code navigation and diagnostics in the gameplay codebase:

* language mode: `gnu17`
* macros: `MODERN=1`, `TESTING=0`, `EMERALD`, `__INTELLISENSE__=1`
* header roots: `include`, project root, and `tools/agbcc/include`

Primary goal: make quoted project includes such as `#include "global.h"` resolve reliably in the editor without changing the actual build pipeline.

### Releasing

`make release` (in WSL) builds `pokeemerald-release.gba` with `RELEASE` defined, which selects the real tuning (e.g. `GUEST_LEADER_CHANCE` 24 instead of the dev value 100). Then `./make_patch.sh <version>` makes `release/fantasy-retold-<version>.bps` (vanilla Emerald → release ROM) with the local RomPatcher.js CLI. The clean ROM and `release/` are gitignored; the patch is uploaded by hand as a GitHub release asset, never the ROM.

### Battle UI graphics folder

Fantasy Retold battle-interface art lives in `graphics/battle_interface_fantasy_retold/` (a fork of upstream's `graphics/battle_interface/`); `src/graphics.c`, `gimmicks.h`, `battle_interface.c` and the level-up banner point there. Concatenated outputs (`textbox.gbapal`, `battle_bar.4bpp`) have their own rules in `graphics_file_rules.mk` under `FRBATINTGFXDIR`.

* **Level-up banner** (`level_up_banner.png`, the slide-in plate for a benched mon that levels up). The PNG's palette is loaded as BG palette 6, so it's also the text palette: name/level print in index 1 with a shadow in 2 (dark `1A1A2E` with a grey `8888A0` shadow, readable on both the cyan top and the white bottom), and the gender symbol uses C/D (♂ blue `3890D8`) and E/F (♀ red `D03040`), both with the grey shadow. The plate's art uses only 0, 3, 5 and A.
* **`L / INNATE` tab** (`move_info_window_l/r.png`). It's drawn with the ability popup's palette (`TAG_ABILITY_POP_UP`), not the PNG's own, so the label uses index 3 (near-black) over index D (yellow). Index 5 is white in that palette and was unreadable there.
* **Party status balls** (`ball_display.png`: healthy, empty, fainted, status). They share the healthbar palette (`TAG_HEALTHBAR_PAL`), so they're drawn on its indices: healthy is a red/black/white Poké Ball, status is the same ball with a purple top, fainted is a filled dark ball, and empty is an outline. Don't make them by swapping the palette onto the vanilla pixels: vanilla's index meanings don't match, and that's how they came out as dark, scrambled blobs.

* **Palettes.** `textbox.gbapal` is built as `textbox_0.gbapal` + `textbox_1.gbapal`, which come from the JASC `textbox_0.pal` / `textbox_1.pal`. Those `.pal` files are written by `generate_jasc_pals.py`; `generate_dark_palettes.py` holds the same colour lists for the raw `.gbapal` outputs. Edit a palette in **both** scripts and rerun `generate_jasc_pals.py` — the build only reads the `.pal` files.
* **Battle menu text** (move names, PP / type, FIGHT / BAG menu) uses the **vanilla** `graphics/battle_interface/text.pal` / `text_pp.pal` (dark grey on white). The dark `text.pal` in the Fantasy Retold folder is no longer used.
* **Menu cursors** (action menu, move menu + move-swap cursors, Yes/No, catch-or-not) are `textbox.png` tiles 1–2 and 28–31, placed on top of those white menu windows. Vanilla draws them in BG palette 0, which is now the dark textbox palette, so the arrows came out navy/blue/red. Their pixels now use `text.pal` indices (14 white, 13 dark grey, 15 light grey), and the `*CreateCursorAt` functions set palette 5 in the tilemap entry (`0x5000 | tile`).

#### Healthbox layout

Drawn from scratch rather than adapted from the vanilla box. Each box is a **dark corner-cut plate** (the same navy as the text box) plus a **status column** beside it.

* **Plate.** Player: 104×32. Row 1 has the name + gender symbol and, right-aligned, the level. Below it, the **HP bar** (4px), a 1px gap, then the **Stamina bar** (2px), both 94px. Each bar's 1px frame is drawn in its fill colour (HP red; Stamina green / yellow, red when exhausted) so both read at their true size on the dark plate. The bottom row has the 1px **EXP track** (singles only) and, right-aligned, `current / effective max` HP, 1px below the Stamina frame (`HB_HP_TEXT_Y`; the digits' ink shadow sits on the plate's ink bottom edge, since the 64×32 doubles sprite leaves no room to make the plate taller). Opponent: 100×25, with the same name row and the same bars at 90px. It has no numbers or EXP, and shows a 5px caught-ball icon before the name when the species is caught. Player doubles plates are the player plate without the EXP track. The HP numbers are always shown with the bars; the vanilla doubles bars/numbers toggle (`SwapHpBarsWithHpText`) is a no-op.
* **Status column.** A box as tall as the plate, with one 12×9 cell per active status line in a fixed order: Soaked, Chill line, Burn, Poison line, Electric line, Sleep line, Confusion line. Each cell holds a 7×7 icon in the line's colour (drop, snowflake, flame, skull, bolt, Z, swirl) and one pip per stage the line can reach, lit bottom-up to the current stage. Cells fill top-down, two per column. Extra columns grow away from the plate: left for the player, right toward the screen centre for the opponent. Up to 6 lines can be active at once (Burn excludes Soaked and the Chill line), which is 3 columns. With no status the column is hidden. `GetHealthboxStatusStages` maps lines to stages: each line shows its status rail's level (`combat.md` §6). It only reads (it runs every frame), so a status that vanilla code wrote straight to `status1` and that no rail has picked up yet shows at the stage its bit stands for (Burn 1, Frozen / Frostbitten → Chill 2, Paralysis → Electric 3, Sleep → Sleep 2, confusion timer → Confusion 1). The column is the old healthbar sprite, now 64×32 on the healthbox palette. Its callback polls the stages every frame and redraws on any change, because confusion and rails change without a healthbox update. It draws with the healthbox palette, so the level-up flash (`FreeHealthboxPalsForLevelUp`) must hand it back that palette, not the stock healthbar one (which re-colored the column after every level-up).
* **Art vs runtime.** `generate_healthbox_art.py` draws only the static parts: plates, bar frames, the 40% tick and the EXP track. Its layout numbers must match `sHealthboxLayouts` / `HB_*` in `battle_interface.c`. Text, bar fills, both bar frames and the 40% tick (also in the art), the exhausted red frame, the ball icon and the status column are drawn at runtime with `FillHealthboxRect` / `PrintOnHealthbox`. These treat the two healthbox sprites as one 128px canvas. The HP bar no longer uses the `hpbar` tiles.
* **Healthbox palette** (`ball_status_bar.png`): 1 plate, 2 empty gauge / cell, 3 ink, 4 slate, 5 white text, 6 grey, 7 red, 8 green, 9 purple, 10 cyan, 11 orange, 12 blue, 13 yellow, 14 sky, 15 pink (Confusion). Text is white with an ink shadow. The gender symbols use explicit colours (blue ♂ / red ♀) instead of the shared dynamic text colours.
* **Positions.** Every plate starts at the sprite's `(x − 32, y − 16)`. The 64×64 player singles box keeps the 64×32 template's centre offset. Gimmick indicators sit just left of the level digits (`sIndicatorPositions` in `battle_gimmick.c`).
* `B_HP_PERCENTAGE_DISPLAY` must stay **FALSE** (a `STATIC_ASSERT` enforces it); the plates have no room for it.
